# The five page types and their skeletons

Pick the type that matches what the reader needs, then follow its skeleton. A page that mixes types (a how-to that drifts into a concept lecture) gets split.

## 1. Concept explainer

Teaches an idea ("What is a prediction market?", "How do prices work?").

- Title as a question
- Opening line: a one-sentence plain definition
- "How it works" section
- One worked example using the product's agreed framing for that concept
- "Why it matters" or "Why people use it" (short)

## 2. How-to / step-by-step

Walks through a task ("How to deposit", "How to sign up").

- Title as a plain action
- Opening line stating the goal
- A short lead-in: "To deposit money:"
- Numbered steps, each starting with the action, with the button or label in **bold**
- A bold inline warning at any irreversible or security step
- A closing line telling them what happens next: "Your balance updates within a few minutes."
- Multiple methods (card vs bank, web vs app) each get a short subsection

## 3. Policy / rules

Explains how something is decided or governed ("How disputes are resolved", "Account limits").

- Title
- Opening line stating the rule in plain terms
- A short overview of the process, start to finish
- Timeframes, conditions, and edge cases stated explicitly
- Cover the whole process — never leave a money-related rule half-explained

## 4. Troubleshooting

Fixes a specific problem ("Why can't I log in?", "Fixing a failed deposit").

- Title naming the exact problem the way the reader sees it
- Opening line: "Try these steps."
- One section per likely cause, most common first
- Numbered fix steps, with device or browser noted if it matters
- Focused on getting them unstuck — no background theory

## 5. FAQ answer

Answers one common question ("Can I cancel an order?", "Are there fees?").

- Title as the literal question
- Opening line gives the full answer in one sentence (yes/no + reason)
- One or two short sentences or a small section expanding it
- Stop once it's answered

## Before and after: one example per type

Weak draft, then a rewrite that follows the standard. The product details are illustrative — swap in the real specifics and the agreed framings.

### 1. Concept explainer

**Before**

> What is a prediction market?
> Prediction markets are a revolutionary financial instrument that has emerged as a powerful mechanism for aggregating decentralized information. By leveraging the wisdom of crowds, participants can express their views on the probability of future events through the buying and selling of outcome shares.

Why it's weak: no answer up front, marketing tone ("revolutionary"), long sentence, unexplained jargon ("instrument", "outcome shares"), no example.

**After**

> What is a prediction market?
> A prediction market lets you trade on what you think will happen.
>
> **How it works**
> Each market asks a question, like "Will it rain in Lagos on Friday?" You buy shares in the outcome you think is right. The price of a share shows how likely people think that outcome is.
> A share that costs 30¢ means about a 30% chance. If that outcome happens, each share pays $1. If it doesn't, the share is worth nothing.
>
> **Why people use it**
> Prices update as people trade, so a market gives you a live read on how likely something is — often more accurate than any single expert.

### 2. How-to / step-by-step

**Before**

> Depositing funds
> In order to fund your account, you will need to navigate to the relevant section of the platform and follow the on-screen prompts to complete the transfer of funds, after which your balance should be updated accordingly.

Why it's weak: vague, no steps, no buttons named, no confirmation of what happens next.

**After**

> How to deposit
> Here's how to add money to your balance.
> To deposit money:
>
> 1. Go to **Wallet** and tap **Add money**.
> 2. Choose how you want to pay: **Card** or **Bank**.
> 3. Enter the amount.
> 4. Check the amount, then tap **Confirm**. **This can't be undone.**
>
> Your balance updates within a few minutes. If it doesn't, refresh the page.

### 3. Policy / rules

**Before**

> Market resolution
> Markets are resolved based on the applicable resolution criteria once the outcome is determined, subject to review.

Why it's weak: too vague to act on; the reader still doesn't know how or when it happens.

**After**

> How markets are settled
> When a market's question is answered in real life, we settle it and pay out the winning shares.
>
> **How it works**
> The event happens (for example, the election is called). We confirm the result using the source listed in the market's rules. Winning shares are paid $1 each. Losing shares are worth nothing.
> Settlement usually happens within 24 hours of the result being clear. A market staying open longer doesn't mean the result is decided — always check the market's rules before you trade.

### 4. Troubleshooting

**Before**

> Login issues
> If you are experiencing difficulties logging in, there may be a number of underlying causes, and we recommend reviewing your setup.

Why it's weak: names no specific cause, gives no actual fix.

**After**

> Why can't I log in?
> Try these steps.
>
> **Check your email and code**
> Make sure you're using the email you signed up with. Enter the latest 6-digit code we sent — older codes stop working. Never share your login code, even with our support team.
>
> **Still stuck?**
> Wait 60 seconds, then request a new code. If it still fails, try a different browser.

### 5. FAQ answer

**Before**

> Is my money safe?
> We take the security of your funds very seriously and employ a range of measures to safeguard your assets at all times.

Why it's weak: doesn't actually answer the question — it reassures vaguely instead.

**After**

> Is my money safe?
> Yes. Your money stays in your account until you choose to trade, and only you can move it.
> You can withdraw your available balance at any time. We never trade against you — when you buy or sell, you're trading with other people in the market.
