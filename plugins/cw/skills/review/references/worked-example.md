# Worked example: findings from one review

A review of a 1,600-word piece for a careers publication, "How I negotiated a 30% raise", whose guide sets a first-person, plain-spoken voice and whose profile requires affiliate disclosure next to any link. Six findings show how levels, severity and proposals fit together.

### R1 — the 30% figure isn't the raise the story describes (BLOCKER, Accuracy)

- **Problem:** The title and opening say 30%; paragraph 9 gives the old and new salaries, which work out to 22%. A reader who repeats the figure is repeating an error.
- **Now:** "How I negotiated a 30% raise"
- **Proposed:** "How I negotiated a 22% raise" — or, if 30% includes the signing bonus, say so in the title's subtitle and in paragraph 9. Question for the author: which?

### R2 — a book link with no disclosure (BLOCKER, Accuracy)

- **Problem:** Paragraph 6 links a negotiation book through an affiliate link; the disclosure sits in the footer. The profile requires disclosure next to the link, and the FTC expects it close to the recommendation.
- **Now:** "This book changed how I think about money."
- **Proposed:** "This book changed how I think about money. (I earn a small commission if you buy it through this link.)"

### R3 — the point arrives in paragraph five (MAJOR, Substantive)

- **Problem:** Four paragraphs of backstory before the reader learns what the piece will give them.
- **Now:** Opens on the writer's first job in 2016.
- **Proposed:** Open on the moment in the meeting where the offer came back; move the 2016 backstory to one sentence in paragraph three. The nut graf — what the reader will be able to do — follows by paragraph two.

### R4 — three quotes in a row from the hiring manager (MINOR, Line)

- **Problem:** The quotes carry the argument the writer should be making.
- **Proposed:** Keep the strongest ("We'd already budgeted more than you asked for"), paraphrase the other two, and add the writer's own reading of what it meant.

### R5 — the publication's sign-off is missing (MINOR, Copy)

- **Problem:** The guide requires every piece to end with its standard question to readers; this one doesn't. A rule the guide settles is never a nit.
- **Proposed:** Add it as the final line.

### R6 — "leverage" where "use" would do (NIT, Line)

- **Problem:** The guide doesn't settle it, and the plainer word fits the voice.
- **Now:** "I leveraged the competing offer."
- **Proposed:** "I used the competing offer." Offered once; the author's call.

### Questions for the author

| #   | Question                                                   | Why it matters        | Where     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------- |
| 1   | Is the 30% salary alone, or salary plus the signing bonus? | Decides R1's fix      | Title, ¶9 |
| 2   | Has the hiring manager agreed to be quoted by name?        | Quoted by name in ¶11 | ¶11       |
