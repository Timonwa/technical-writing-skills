# The agreement for each kind

Before anyone writes, get the terms in writing — an email both sides reply to is enough where there's no contract. The [Authors Guild's legal guide to periodical contracts](https://authorsguild.org/app/uploads/2022/11/Chapter-12_Writers-Legal-Guide.pdf) treats the document an editor sends as "an offer" whose "terms are open to negotiation"; the same holds for any agreement here. Each item is a line to confirm; record the answers in the topic's `brief.md` and the row in `pitch-log.md`'s **Agreements**. This names what to look for, not what to sign: legal advice on a specific contract is a lawyer's or a writers' organisation's job.

## Every kind

- [ ] **Scope and angle** — the approved outline, the reader, anything added or cut.
- [ ] **Length and dates** — the draft date, any outline checkpoint, the publish date.
- [ ] **Byline, bio, headshot and links** — what the bio may say, how many links, whether they're followed.
- [ ] **Editing and approval** — how many rounds, and whether the writer sees the final edit and the title before it goes out.
- [ ] **Rights** — which rights, where, in what formats, for how long, and when the piece may be republished elsewhere.
- [ ] **Code and assets** — who hosts a demo repository and under what licence (`cw:demo-project`); who supplies images.

## Writing for them

- [ ] **Fee and when it's paid** — on acceptance or on publication, and how (invoice, platform, bank transfer). Judge it per word or per piece against the outlet's published rates and what other writers report it pays.
- [ ] **Payment terms** — days after the invoice; 30 days ("net 30") is the usual term where none is stated (common practice).
- [ ] **Exclusivity** — a period starting on publication is normal; the Guild calls its duration "the key point", because later uses of the piece have value. Avoid granting all rights or work for hire unless the fee reflects it; the Guild notes a piece isn't a work made for hire unless you expressly agree in writing that it is.
- [ ] **Reversion** — rights return to the writer if the outlet doesn't publish within an agreed time.
- [ ] **Kill fee** — what's paid if the outlet commissions the piece and doesn't run it. The Guild recommends "50 percent (or higher) of the agreed fee", with expenses reimbursed in full, and prefers payment on acceptance that's nonrefundable.

## Swap

- [ ] **The two pieces** — each side's topic, format (blog post, newsletter issue) and length.
- [ ] **The two dates** — and what happens if one side's piece is late: the other waits, or runs on schedule.
- [ ] **Links back** — the author's bio link on each piece, and any extra link between the two publications.
- [ ] **Promotion** — what each side shares, where and when (a social post, a newsletter mention).
- [ ] **Rights** — each piece first published by its host, exclusive for an agreed period, then the author's to republish with a link to the original.
- [ ] **Guidelines** — each side sends its contributor guidelines and a brief ([template](contributor-guidelines.md)).

## Co-written

- [ ] **Who writes which part** — sections, or one drafts and one edits, and who has the final say on a disagreement.
- [ ] **Whose guide it follows** — the first publisher's, unless agreed point by point.
- [ ] **Where it runs first** and the date; then **the copy on the other platform** — a full copy or an opening excerpt, its date, and a canonical link to the original (`cw:cross-posting`).
- [ ] **Bylines** — both names and the order, or one name "with" the other; a bio and link for each.
- [ ] **Rights** — both authors may link to it and quote it; neither republishes it elsewhere without the other's agreement.
- [ ] **Money** — any fee, sponsorship or affiliate income, and how it's split, or none.

## Guest on ours

- [ ] **Paid or unpaid** — the fee and when it's paid.
- [ ] **Rights you ask for** — first publication, exclusive for a period; after it, the guest may republish with a link to your original.
- [ ] **Originality** — unpublished anywhere else; how AI use is disclosed.
- [ ] **Edit rounds** — the guest sees the final edit and the title before it goes out.
- [ ] **No paid links** — the guest discloses any connection to a product the piece mentions; a paid placement inside a guest piece is a sponsorship and follows its terms.

## Sponsored

- [ ] **Deliverables** — the slot, issue or post; its length; the number and wording of links; any image or logo; where the slot sits in the issue.
- [ ] **Dates** — the send or publish date, the date the sponsor's materials are due, and the date of each approval round.
- [ ] **Who writes it and approval rounds** — you write it in your own voice; the sponsor checks facts and approves in an agreed number of rounds. A sponsor who approves the copy has control over it, which in the UK makes it an advertorial under the CAP Code ([sponsorship.md](sponsorship.md)).
- [ ] **What you won't say** — no claims you can't check, no comparisons you haven't made, nothing from "What it will never do" in `profile.md`; the sponsor's required claims are checked before they run.
- [ ] **The disclosure label** — its wording and where it sits, agreed in writing and never removed ([sponsorship.md](sponsorship.md)).
- [ ] **Regulator approval** — for readers in Nigeria, who obtains ARCON's approval for the advert, where it applies ([sponsorship.md](sponsorship.md)).
- [ ] **Payment** — the price, the currency, a deposit or payment in full before it runs, the invoice terms.
- [ ] **Cancellation** — the notice either side gives, and what's paid if the sponsor cancels late or you can't run it.
- [ ] **Exclusivity** — whether a competitor may sponsor the same issue or the next ones.
- [ ] **The results report** — which figures, by when.
- [ ] **How long it stays up** — a sponsored post's links and label stay for as long as the post is live.
