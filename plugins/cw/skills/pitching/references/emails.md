# Email templates

One template per email. Copy the block, fill every `<blank>` from the brief, `profile.md`, `media-kit.md` and the partner's guidelines, and keep a line whose blank isn't known yet until it is — never drop it silently. Cut a line only where the template marks it optional. Each email stays readable without scrolling: about 150–300 words (common practice), within any cap the partner sets. Write in the publication's voice from `writing-guide.md`; the structure is fixed, the wording isn't.

After sending, add or update the row in `pitch-log.md`.

## Pitch — writing for them

```text
To: <editor's name and address, from the guidelines>
Subject: Pitch: <the proposed title or the idea in a few words>

Hi <editor's first name>,

<The idea in one or two sentences: what the reader will be able to do or understand.>

<Why this outlet's readers: who among them needs it, and how it differs from what the outlet has run.>

<Why now — optional: a release, a change, a common question.>

<Why you, in one or two lines: first-hand experience, a project, data only you have.>

Proposed title: <title>
Outline:
- <section>
- <section>
- <section>

Length and delivery: <words>, by <date>.
Published work: <link>, <link>, <link>
<Simultaneous pitch — only when the guidelines allow it: "I've sent this idea to one other outlet and will withdraw it the moment one accepts.">

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Follow-up

Once, after the partner's stated response time, or after about two weeks if none is stated (common practice).

```text
To: <same address>
Subject: Re: <original subject>

Hi <first name>,

Following up on the <pitch or proposal> below, sent <date>: <the idea in one line>. <Why it's timely, if a date matters.>

If it isn't a fit, a quick no is welcome and I'll take it elsewhere.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Swap proposal

```text
To: <partner's name and address>
Subject: A swap: <your topic> for your readers, <their topic> for mine

Hi <first name>,

<Why you're writing to them: a piece of theirs you read, and the overlap between their readers and yours, in one or two sentences.>

The idea: I write <format: a blog post / a newsletter issue> for <their publication> on <your topic>, and you write one for <your publication> on <the topic you'd like from them, or "a topic of your choice that my readers would want">.

About my readers: <who they are, and the numbers worth naming, with the date measured>.

What each of us gets: <a bio and a link to the author's site / a social mention / a newsletter mention>, on both sides.

Timing: <both pieces in the same week / your date and mine>.

I'll send our contributor guidelines and a short brief if you're in.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Co-writing proposal

```text
To: <co-author's name and address>
Subject: Co-writing <the piece's working title>?

Hi <first name>,

<The piece in one or two sentences, and why it's better written by both of you than by either alone.>

How I'd split it: <who writes which part — or one drafts and one edits>.

Where it runs: first on <your blog / their blog / your newsletter>, by <date>; then <a full copy / an opening excerpt> on <the other platform>, pointing to the original.

Bylines: <both names, in this order / one name "with" the other>, with a bio and link for each.

<What you need from them: their data, their example, their review — optional.>

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Reply to an incoming pitch

Three versions; send one. Reply within the response time your contributor guidelines state, or within two weeks (common practice).

**Accept**

```text
Subject: Re: <their subject>

Hi <first name>,

Yes — <the piece> is a fit for <publication>'s readers, <one line on why>.

What we'd like: <the angle, as agreed or adjusted>, about <words>, by <draft date>.

Terms: <fee and when it's paid, or unpaid>; <the rights you ask for: first publication, exclusive for <period>, then theirs to republish with a link back>; <the number of edit rounds>; a byline, a bio of <length> and <number> link(s).

Our contributor guidelines and the brief: <link or attached>. Reply to confirm and I'll put it in the calendar.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

**Ask to revise**

```text
Subject: Re: <their subject>

Hi <first name>,

<What works in the pitch, in one line.> It's close, but <what stops it being a fit: the angle, the overlap with <a published piece>, the reader>.

Would you send a revised pitch that <the specific change>? Our contributor guidelines are here: <link>.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

**Decline**

```text
Subject: Re: <their subject>

Hi <first name>,

Thanks for thinking of <publication>. We'll pass on this one: <the reason in one line — it doesn't fit our readers / we've covered it recently / we're not taking guest posts right now>.

<Optional: what would fit, or where it might.>

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Sponsor pitch

```text
To: <the person who runs sponsorships or partnerships at the brand>
Subject: Sponsoring <publication>: <the reader> who <what they'd want from the brand>

Hi <first name>,

<Why this brand: a product your readers use or ask about, in one or two sentences — never a claim you haven't checked.>

<Publication> reaches <who reads it>. As of <date measured>: <subscribers>, <open rate>, <click rate>, <monthly readers>.

What you could book: <the slot, issue or post that fits them>, from <price> — the full options are in the media kit: <link or attached>.

Every sponsored placement is written in <publication>'s voice, labelled <the disclosure label>, and followed by a results report.

Dates open: <the next available dates>.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Reply to a sponsor

When a brand approaches you. Two versions; send one.

**Interested**

```text
Subject: Re: <their subject>

Hi <first name>,

Thanks for reaching out. <One line on why the product fits your readers, or the question you need answered to know.>

The media kit has the audience, the numbers (measured <date>), the slots and the rate card: <link or attached>.

Before we book: <the questions — the product or link to feature, the dates they want, who approves the copy>.

Every sponsored placement is written in <publication>'s voice and labelled <the disclosure label>; <what you won't do, from the profile>.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

**Decline**

```text
Subject: Re: <their subject>

Hi <first name>,

Thanks for thinking of <publication>. It isn't a fit for our readers, so we'll pass.

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```

## Sponsor results report

Sent within the time the agreement sets, or within a week of the placement (common practice). Every figure comes from the platform or the analytics tool, with the date it was read; a figure you don't have is left blank and said, never estimated.

```text
To: <sponsor contact>
Subject: Results: <brand> in <publication>, <date of the placement>

Hi <first name>,

Here's how <the slot / issue / post> did, as of <date read>.

| Measure                     | Result |
| --------------------------- | ------ |
| Placement                   |        |
| Sent or published on        |        |
| Delivered to                |        |
| Opens (unique)              |        |
| Clicks on your link (unique) |        |
| Click rate                  |        |
| Page views (a post)         |        |
| Replies or comments         |        |

<What readers said, if anything: quoted with permission, or summarised.>

<Open rates are inflated by mail clients that load images automatically, so clicks are the better measure — optional.>

<The next open dates, if they'd book again — optional.>

<Sign-off>
<Name>
```
