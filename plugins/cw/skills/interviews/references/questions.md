# Questions that work

Question sets for each kind of person and each output, and an example of checking facts back. Adapt them to the person and the piece; ask the must-ask ones first.

## By who you're interviewing

### An expert or researcher

- What do people outside your field assume that isn't so?
- Where's the number behind that — the study, the dataset? Can I see it?
- What would change your mind?

### A customer or user

- What were you doing before, and what made you look for something else?
- What did it cost you — in hours, money or missed orders — before the change? Where's that figure from?
- What was harder than you expected when you started?
- What's different now, and how do you know? Can you check the number in your own records?

### Someone with a personal or lived experience

- Where would you like to start?
- What do you remember about that day — where were you, who was there?
- What do you wish people understood about it?
- Is there anything you'd rather I didn't include?

### A founder or business owner

- What were you doing the week you decided to start?
- What nearly ended it, and what did you do?
- What do you sell, and who pays for it? (Their stake, which the reader is told.)
- What would you tell someone starting the same business today?

### A community member, event guest or attendee

- What brought you here, and how long have you been coming?
- What's one moment from today you'll remember?
- What would you change about it?

### Another creator

- Walk me through how you made your last piece, from the idea to publishing.
- What do you do that most people in your niche don't?
- Is there anything between us I should tell readers about — a collaboration, a link, a swap?

## By what it becomes

### Quotes in a feature or story

- How did you get here — what changed your mind, and when?
- Tell me about a specific time this went wrong, or right. Who was there, and what happened next?
- What would you do differently if you started again?

### A Q&A article

The questions are printed, so each one stands alone, reads well, and earns its place.

- One opening question a reader would ask first: "You run 50 miles a week alongside a full-time job. When do you train?"
- One question per idea; no question that only works because of the answer before it.
- A question the reader wouldn't think to ask, and the one they're most curious about.
- A closing question that looks forward: "What are you training for next?"

### A profile or feature

- What do you spend most of your week actually doing?
- What's a decision you made that others thought was wrong?
- Who taught you the thing you rely on most?
- Who else should I talk to about you? (A profile needs people who know them.)

### A case study

- The problem: what wasn't working, and what did it cost?
- What they did: why this, what changed, how long it took, what went wrong.
- The result: what's different, in numbers from their own records, with the period and the before figure.
- May we name you and your business, and who approves it for the business?

### A testimonial

- What would you tell a friend who was thinking about it?
- What was it like before, and what's different now?
- In a sentence or two, how would you put it? (Their words, approved as written.)

### An opinion or argument

- What's the strongest argument against what you're saying?
- Who would disagree with you, and what would they say?
- What evidence would change your mind?

## Reality checks

- How do you know? Who else saw it?
- Is that true everywhere, or where you are?
- What's changing soon enough that I shouldn't present it as permanent?
- Is anything you've told me under embargo, or not for publication?
- Who else should I talk to?
- What didn't I ask that I should have?

## A read-back that checks facts, not wording

> Thanks for Tuesday. Before this runs on Friday, I'd like to check four facts — not your quotes, which are from the recording:
>
> 1. You opened the first kitchen in 2019, in a former bakery — right?
> 2. Your team is 14 people now, up from three.
> 3. The sourdough starter is fed twice a day, at 6 am and 6 pm.
> 4. Could you send your head chef's name exactly as it should be spelled?
