# Case study

One customer's story: their problem, what they did, and the result, with numbers they confirm. It goes in the format file the brief lists — usually `blog.md`, after the front matter — and the notes file in `interviews/` keeps the transcript, the approval and where each number came from. Copy both blocks below. Italic text is a hint; replace it. Keep every field, and leave one blank until it's known rather than removing it.

## Before publishing

Kept in the notes file, under the consent table.

```markdown
| Field             | Answer                                                                                       |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer          | _the person's name and role, and the business — as they approved it_                         |
| What they used    | _the product or service, and since when_                                                     |
| Connection        | _anything they were given for taking part — a discount, a free plan, a fee — or none_        |
| Numbers confirmed | _each figure, the period it covers, where it came from, and who confirmed it_                |
| Approved by       | _who approved the finished draft, for the business and for the person, and the date_         |
```

## The case study

```markdown
_The title, in the front matter: who they are and what changed — "How a two-person bakery cut missed orders"._

_One-paragraph summary: the customer, the problem and the headline result, with its number._

## The problem

_What wasn't working, in their words where you can, and what it cost them — time, money, customers, sleep. The before figure goes here._

## What they did

_Why they chose this, what they changed, how long it took, and what went wrong on the way — a case study with no snags reads as an advert._

## The result

_The numbers they confirmed, each with its period and its before figure — "missed orders down from about 12 a week to 2 over three months", not "orders soared". What didn't change, too._

## In their words

_One or two quotes, approved word for word._

_Disclosure, where there's a connection: "Shade Bakes received three months of the service free for taking part."_
```

## What makes it hold up

- **Every number comes from the customer's own records** — their order book, their accounts, their dashboard — and they confirm it; a figure from the company's sales team is checked with the customer before it's used (common practice).
- **A gap stays visible.** Where the customer can't give a number, the result is described without one, never estimated for them.
- **One customer's result is theirs**: the case study says what happened to them, not what every reader will get (common practice).
- **A connection is disclosed beside the story** — anything of value given for taking part ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)).

## Sign-off

Send the finished draft whole with the [approval email](emails.md#approval), to the person and, where the business is named, whoever can approve for it. Nothing is published until the approval is recorded in the notes file (common practice).
