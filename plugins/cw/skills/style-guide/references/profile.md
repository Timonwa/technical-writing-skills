# The publication profile

The profile says _who_ a publication is, _who_ it's for and _why_ — the facts a writing guide rarely holds. Fill it from the owner, not from the published pieces, and revisit it when the audience or the purpose changes.

## The publication

- **Name**, and what it is in one line.
- **Its channels** — a blog, a newsletter, both — and the language it's written in.
- **Who writes it**, and whether pieces carry a byline.

## Who's behind it

- What they do, why they're worth reading on this subject, and how they want to come across.

## Who it's for

- Who the readers are, what they already know, and what always needs explaining.
- Why they came, and what they're wary of.
- **Who it is not for.** Naming that sharpens every decision downstream — what to explain, what to cut, which pitch to decline.

## What it's for

- The job it does for readers, and the job it does for its owner.
- What success looks like, and the one action a piece should produce.
- The lines it will never cross.

## Channels

Per channel:

- **Cadence** — how often, and on what day.
- **For a newsletter**: the platform, the signup promise in its exact words, what an issue contains, and the sender name and address.

## Defaults

What a new topic starts with, so `cw:content-planning` can offer each one as the answer to a brief's question instead of asking from scratch. A brief may depart from any default; the default only changes when the owner says the publication's habit has.

- **A new topic becomes** — the formats a topic usually turns into: a blog post, a newsletter issue, social posts, cross-posts, a demo project, or any mix.
- **First published on** — where the original lives: the publication's own blog, or the platform it's written on.
- **Newsletter** — whether the newsletter sends every new post, a digest of them, only its own issues, or there's no newsletter.
- **Cross-posts** — one line per platform the publication copies to, and whether it takes the full copy or the opening excerpt.
- **Social platforms** — one line per platform it shares on, with the account's handle.
- **Cover image** — the cover template in `brand.md`, or none.

## Obligations

- **What it discloses, and where** — affiliate links, sponsorship, gifted products.
- **Claims that need a source or a qualified review** — health, finance, legal — and who reviews them.
- **Who approves a piece** before it's published.
- **The accessibility standard it holds to** — alt text, captions, reading level.

## Example

> **Who it's for.** People in their first five years of work who want to earn more and haven't negotiated before. They know their job well; they don't know how pay is set. Not for: hiring managers, or anyone looking for investment advice. Confirmed by the owner 2026-04-02.
